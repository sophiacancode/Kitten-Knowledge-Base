Social enrichment gives cats opportunities for comfortable interaction with people or other animals they get along with. With people, this might mean playing, enjoying gentle petting, or spending time nearby without being handled.

## Positive Interactions

- Share short play sessions with appropriate toys.
- Offer gentle petting when your cat welcomes it.
- Try brushing if your cat enjoys being groomed.
- Give your cat quiet time nearby without expecting physical contact.

### Let Your Cat Set the Pace

Sit near your cat without crowding them and wait to see whether they approach. If they welcome petting, keep it brief and pause to give them a chance to continue or leave.

If your cat retreats to a hiding place, leave them undisturbed. Spending time together does not have to mean touching or holding them.

> Offer attention, but leave your cat free to walk away.

## Relationships with Other Cats

Another cat is not automatically a solution to boredom. Some cats get along well with feline companions, while others are more comfortable without them.

When introducing cats, take the process gradually rather than putting them together immediately. Provide separate resources and places to retreat so they are not forced to share every space.
