Sensory enrichment gives cats things to watch, sniff, touch, and investigate. A cardboard box or a moving toy can offer something to explore without rearranging the entire room.
## Ideas to Explore

- Offer a cardboard box for sniffing, rubbing, and exploring.
- Move a toy along the floor during supervised play.
- Rotate toys so you can reintroduce them rather than leaving every toy out at once.

### Let the Cat Choose

Introduce one activity at a time and let your cat approach at their own pace. Leave familiar resting places available so they can move away if they prefer.

For example, place a box where your cat can investigate it, rather than putting the cat inside. They may explore it immediately, return later, or ignore it.

> Give your cat something to investigate, not something they have to interact with.

## Finding What Interests Your Cat

Watch what your cat chooses to do. Do they follow moving toys, investigate boxes, or spend more time searching for food? Use those observations to choose activities rather than assuming every cat will enjoy the same things.
