Predatory play lets cats practice hunting behaviors such as stalking, chasing, and pouncing. Interactive play involves a person moving a toy. A cat chasing a wand toy is doing both: practicing hunting movements and playing with someone.

## Choosing Toys

- Use a wand toy to imitate moving prey.
- Offer toy mice or balls for chasing and batting.
- Try different toys to find out which ones your cat prefers.

### Using a Wand Toy

1. Move the toy along the floor, keeping it away from your hands and feet.
2. Pause occasionally so your cat has time to watch and stalk it.
3. Let your cat pounce and catch the toy rather than keeping it out of reach throughout the game.
4. Keep sessions short and offer them regularly.

Allowing your cat to catch the toy gives the chase an outcome and can help prevent frustration.

## Playing Safely

Avoid using fingers or toes as toys. This can teach cats to direct play toward people's hands and feet, which becomes more painful as kittens grow.

Check toys for damage and put string and wand toys away after supervised play.

> Make the toy the target—not your fingers or toes.
